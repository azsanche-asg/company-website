Evidence & evaluation

Match the guarantee to the clinical task.

Reference-label inclusion, additional grading and finding verification use different error definitions. Their results should be read separately.

Chest X-ray

The CheXpert benchmark evaluates how often an output set misses the expert reference across all in-scope studies. At the 5% target, its 60-study evaluation produced 8 single outputs, 52 unresolved outputs and no reference misses.

This is not a guarantee conditional on single outputs. The NIH ChestX-ray14 images used for public practice are separate from this benchmark.

Fundus and OCT

The fundus example concerns errors among images eligible to skip additional grading. The OCT example concerns incorrect findings eligible to skip additional verification.

The images are real public research samples. Their policy scores and workload outcomes are prepared for the walkthrough, with no calculated bound or clinical validation for those populations.

Evaluate the workflow, not just the score

Compare the product’s current process with the proposed process on the same independent data. Report errors, unresolved outputs, retained checks and clinical effort together. Keep patient-level decisions and existing referral or alert rules in view.

Assess patient and site dependence, device and acquisition differences, source quality and the intended reference. A change of population can require reassessment even when the model version stays the same.

Scope, effort and what is demonstrated today

The public walkthroughs are local demonstrations with fictional clinical interfaces. Chest X-ray replays a separate historical calculation; fundus and OCT use prepared outcomes. Clinical connectors, production monitoring and patient-level workflow automation are not provided by these demos.

Existing reviewed examples may be reusable after checks on scope, model version, reference definitions, patient overlap and prior use. Score preparation, labeling, adjudication, integration tests and ongoing audit effort must be scoped. There is no universal label count, installation time or guaranteed saving.

One image or B-scan does not complete an encounter or examination. Clinical owners retain responsibility for ungradable inputs, referral decisions, final reports and follow-up.
