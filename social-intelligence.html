What exactly would the guarantee cover?

The mistake: a mention that meets your agreed priority definition is routed out of immediate review. Later discovery during an audit does not erase the original routing mistake.

The headline’s denominator: all genuinely priority mentions in the defined input population. The limit concerns the fraction of those mentions missed by immediate review.

The statement: a statistical risk bound under the assessment’s assumptions. It is not a promise about every mention, a fixed count in every batch, every crisis or every public post. Collection coverage and incident response are separate.

The conditions: relevant reference labels, an appropriate score, an agreed sampling procedure, compatible model and priority-rule versions, and future inputs consistent with the assessed population. Reposts, shared incidents and changes in context matter. A change may require more review and reassessment.

Our foundation is conformal prediction and conformal risk control. The method and evidence must support the particular risk claimed; support for one risk does not certify another.

Two questions, two denominators

Make the quality commitment specific.

The headline promise

Of the mentions needing attention,
how many miss immediate review?

Priority mentions routed to the routine queue, divided by all genuinely priority mentions.

This measures how much of the important content the routing policy misses.

A complementary control

Of the mentions in the routine queue,
how many actually need attention?

Priority mentions routed to the routine queue, divided by all mentions routed there.

This measures the priority content within the group removed from immediate review.

A guarantee covering both requires evidence for both and an agreed joint confidence level. Neither percentage is a crisis-detection guarantee.

What would we connect?

The mention and source reference, score and model version, priority definition, scope information and an existing queue decision point. Retain a record of the policy and original destination for review and audit.

What happens when conditions change?

Missing inputs, timeouts, unsupported scope or a suspended policy keep new mentions in immediate review. Source, language, model or priority-rule changes may require reassessment.

New incidents can also change what matters without changing the model version. Audits take time to reveal changes; monitoring does not promise immediate detection.

What you bring

One workflow. Its evidence.

  • The listening provider, brand or topic, sources, languages and review volumes.
  • Original mention records and context, priority scores, model versions and routing history.
  • Reference labels and sampling history, including evidence about routine mentions.
  • Your priority definition, acceptable missed-priority risk and current rules.
  • An operational owner, analyst lead and product or technical contact.

What the evaluation delivers

A basis for a deployment decision.

  • The supported risk bound and scope, where the evidence permits one.
  • Separate results for priority misses and priority content in the routine queue.
  • A held-out comparison of current controls, the score alone and calibrated policy.
  • Immediate review, audits and net analyst effort at the supported limit.
  • Integration, monitoring and ownership requirements, with a recommendation on a monitored pilot or further preparation.

No fixed saving, label count or deployment time is assumed. The evidence may support useful automation, require further preparation or show that immediate review should remain.
