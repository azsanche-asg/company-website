What exactly is controlled?

Two risks answer
different business questions.

Priority mentions missed

Priority mentions not sent for immediate review ÷ all genuinely priority mentions.

This is the risk named by the headline. A later audit finding does not rewrite the original routing decision.

Priority in the routine queue

Priority mentions not sent for immediate review ÷ all mentions routed to the routine queue.

This describes the priority content in the group removed from immediate review. It does not establish the first risk.

A statistical procedure must support the specific risk claimed and account for policy selection. Supporting both risks requires an agreed joint confidence statement. An observed rate within a target is insufficient.

What population and conditions does the statement depend on?

Define the source stream, languages, time/context, model, priority rules and reference-label procedure. The denominator concerns mentions in that agreed population, not every post on the internet or every real-world incident.

Human reference labels describe an agreed operational priority. They do not prove the truth of every underlying event. Reposts, linked conversations and repeated incidents can create dependence that must be reflected in sampling and the statistical method.

A population-wide statement does not automatically certify each brand, language or incident subgroup. Changed scope or context may need new evidence; monitoring can detect changes only after relevant information arrives.

Calculated research / HumAID

A bound on priority content
in the reduced-review group.

The public crisis-information benchmark evaluates priority mentions among those removed from immediate review. At the 5% target, 1,327 of 9,559 test mentions were selected; 45 were priority mentions, an observed 3.39%.

The calibration upper bound was 3.85% at 95% confidence under the study’s declared assumptions. Observed priority retention was 99.28%; that second quantity was not certified by this experiment.

These are benchmark results. They do not establish commercial listening savings or a customer-specific missed-priority guarantee.

How should I interpret the demonstration’s numbers?

The 1,000-mention replay is separate from the short example, current six-item walkthrough and prepared calibration cohort. Its counts explain a workflow, not an independently measured result.

The comparison with an example score filter is illustrative and is not a tuned customer baseline. Review counts do not directly establish time or cost savings. A real comparison must use the same held-out population and account for ongoing work.

Relay and Signaldesk are illustrative integration and analyst applications. No external listening provider is connected.

Your own evidence

Establish the limit.
Compare the work it requires.

  1. 01 / Define

    Agree the priority and population.

    Name the brand or topic, sources and languages. Agree how impact and urgency determine which mentions need immediate attention and how reference disagreements are resolved.

  2. 02 / Prepare

    Inspect evidence across the stream.

    Include routine as well as flagged mentions. Preserve original scores, versions, routing and sampling history. Account for reposts, shared incidents and repeated conversations.

  3. 03 / Assess

    Calibrate and evaluate independently.

    Assess score suitability, separate development, calibration and evaluation, and use a procedure appropriate to the chosen risk. Compare current controls, the score alone and calibrated policy on held-out mentions.

  4. 04 / Decide

    Measure operational usefulness.

    Inspect the supported bound, coverage of the scope, ongoing review work and operating costs before recommending a monitored pilot or further preparation.

Use the evidence you already have

Existing reviews may help.
The sampling history matters.

Assess source/context access, label definitions and quality, original scores, model and rule versions, and how records were selected for review.

A labelled collection of only urgent or flagged mentions cannot reveal how many priority mentions were routed away. Evidence from the routine group and a suitable sampling design are needed to assess misses.

Other brands, topics, languages, sources or incident conditions may require additional evidence. No fixed review count can be promised before assessing the scope and chosen limit.

The operating value

Measure useful review reduction.
Include the ongoing work.

Compare against the current priority rules and thresholds. Count immediate review avoided, audits added, remaining routine-queue inspection, investigations and recurring monitoring. Record setup and reassessment separately.

Measure analyst time as well as counts. A short routine check and an investigation are different tasks. If the same mentions must still be read for reporting or analysis, count only the work actually removed.

Inspect both risk denominators alongside workload. Faster routing does not establish faster incident resolution; response remains the organisation’s responsibility.

fallback and owners.

EyeTrustAI

Provider or technical owner

Connect and operate score and queue interfaces, version binding, decision records, activation, pauses and review fallback.

Customer and analyst team

Define priority, supply or adjudicate reference labels, conduct ongoing review and audits, and own escalation and incident response.

What would the integration need to agree?

Input stream and source/context access; score and priority-rule identity; allowed routing action and retained destinations; policy and decision records; sampling for audits; throughput and latency; data access and retention; named owners for investigation, suspension and restart.

Missing permission, unavailable scoring or unsupported scope must retain immediate review. A new supported policy still needs explicit activation. The demonstration does not establish a production API, deployment arrangement or compatibility with a specific vendor.
