04 / Research collection

Medical segmentation

Chákṣu → RIGA

How much expert-defined anatomy can a predicted region omit, and how large must the region become to limit that omission?

Studies of optic-cup regions examine expert disagreement, reference omission, region size, and transfer with one frozen scorer.

Explore the public repository ↗

Selected finding

Calibration transfer has a spatial cost.

Expected image-average omission relative to expert masks. Source certificates do not automatically transfer. These results establish no patient-level, worst-expert, or clinical deployment guarantee.

How to read this evidence

Every result has a scope.

These public research artifacts originated in EyeTrustAI’s development research. They make calibration calculations and aggregate findings reproducible; they do not distribute the production platform.

Targets and observations are distinct
A statistical target is different from the error observed in one finite evaluation cohort. Both are reported.
References define the question
Human labels, pathology references, and expert contours establish different kinds of evidence.
Reproduction boundaries are explicit
Calibration replay and aggregate report generation do not reconstruct every historical model prediction or source-data record.

Our research direction

Statistical guarantees
in a changing world.

We investigate how risk guarantees can remain meaningful as AI systems encounter new data, receive incomplete feedback, and adapt.

01 / Changing environments

Where does the evidence still apply?

Understanding the scope of calibration across populations and contexts, and when new evidence is required.

02 / Adapting systems

What happens after deployment?

Studying uncertainty through sequential decisions, delayed feedback, and interactions that change the operating environment.

Ongoing research directions. These are open scientific questions, not claims of available product guarantees.
