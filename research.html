What this establishes for the walkthrough

HumAID supplies benchmark evidence for one of its two risks. The fictional product-safety walkthrough also requires a limit on priority mentions missed, among all priority mentions. HumAID does not establish that joint guarantee, validate the commercial workflow, or measure customer savings.

Population, labels and sampling assumptions

The primary source policy used 4,774 final-calibration labels and a separate locked evaluation of 9,559 posts from the 2018 event mixture. “Priority” follows seven HumAID information categories; its human-agreed labels are research references, not verified incident truth.

The exact-binomial analysis relies on its sampling assumptions. Splitting posts into roles does not establish independence: reposts, shared incidents and changing event mixtures can undermine it. The pooled result is not a guarantee for each event; the Kerala evaluation slice had 6.10% selected error.

The separately calibrated 2019 study uses another population. Its result is not a matched estimate of the benefit of recalibration.

Read the protocol & assumptions ↗
How the two-risk walkthrough differs

The walkthrough uses a synthetic 1,000-mention replay to explain workload and errors. Its separate prepared cohort illustrates an assessment process; neither generates a certificate in the browser.

Priority mentions missed: missed priority mentions divided by all priority mentions. Priority in the routine queue: those same missed priority mentions divided by all mentions leaving immediate review.

The illustrated joint profile assigns a 2.5% statistical failure allowance to each risk, for at most 5% combined failure under valid testing and selection assumptions. Its grouped approach would require an explicit sampling plan, bounded group sizes and defensible independence between situations. The prepared group count does not establish those assumptions.

A real deployment needs evidence for its own scorer, priority rules and population. The walkthrough’s confidence statement is a simulated policy status, separate from HumAID’s measured single-risk evidence.

What a stronger baseline comparison would require

The walkthrough’s example score filter is not tuned to the same limits. The benchmark’s raw-score baseline is also not evidence of superiority over a carefully tuned alternative.

A fair evaluation would use the same frozen scorer, scope and adjudicated labels; split related mentions by incident; tune both approaches on separate development/calibration data to the same limits; then compare workload–error curves once on untouched evaluation groups. Audit effort, setup and reassessment costs, uncertainty and subgroup results belong in the report.

No superiority result is claimed for the fictional commercial workflow. A matched study requires scored mentions, reference labels, timestamps, source/language scope, incident/repost grouping and measured review effort.

Assumptions, image sources & the comparison still needed

The formal claim concerns expected loss over calibration and a new study under exchangeability, with a frozen scorer and bounded monotone loss family. It is not a 95% bound for every fitted rule. Multiple views are aggregated per study; repeated studies may still share a patient. Upstream scorer selection on the benchmark has not been independently ruled out.

The six visible chest-X-ray examples are NIH ChestX-ray14 images with fresh model scores. They are not members of the CheXpert benchmark, and its guarantee does not transfer to them. The public research package reproduces aggregate calculations; the walkthrough replays archived benchmark scores.

A matched comparison should tune a confidence/abstention alternative on development data, freeze both methods, and evaluate on the same separate patient-grouped cohort at comparable output or error rates. A reader study would then measure time, corrections and reliance on AI. Those studies have not been performed here.

What a clinical evaluation would need

For fundus and OCT, the 1% limit and 95% confidence setting are examples. A real selected-output guarantee needs a valid assessment method, a frozen selection rule and an appropriate sampling unit. Multiple images, eyes, B-scans and visits from the same patient require dependence-aware design; a pooled result does not guarantee every subgroup.

Compare the current workflow, a tuned alternative and EyeTrustAI using the same model outputs and independently reviewed cases. Keep related records together across development and final evaluation. Predeclare thresholds, endpoints and repeat-assessment rules, then report errors, review work and uncertainty on the held-out cohort.

For fundus, include sensitivity, referral workload, ungradable images and encounter completion. For OCT, include error directions, final-report corrections, reader time and reliance on preliminary findings. Record audit effort separately from setup and reassessment costs. The walkthroughs outline these comparisons; they do not report a completed trial.

How to read this evidence

Every result has a scope.

These public research artifacts originated in EyeTrustAI’s development research. They make calibration calculations and aggregate findings reproducible; they do not distribute the production platform.

Targets and observations are distinct
A statistical target is different from the error observed in one finite evaluation cohort. Both are reported.
References define the question
Human labels, pathology references, and expert contours establish different kinds of evidence.
Reproduction boundaries are explicit
Calibration replay and aggregate report generation do not reconstruct every historical model prediction or source-data record.
