What exactly would the guarantee cover?

The error: a defined material wrong or unsupported claim in an original AI draft. The definition and reference sources are agreed for the workflow.

The population: the original replies selected by the policy for automation, before pre-send audits or human corrections. The error rate among replies actually sent after audits is a separate quantity.

The statement: a statistical bound on that population’s defined error risk, using a method that supports selection and under its sampling assumptions. It is not a promise of correctness for each reply or a fixed error count in every batch.

The conditions: relevant reviewed evidence, a fixed model and policy scope, and future cases compatible with the assessment’s assumptions. Changed models, source policies, languages or request mix may require reassessment. Some targets support no automation.

What would integration require?

Access to the complete original draft and trusted source records before sending, a way to hold delivery, and an existing human review route. A feed of already-sent transcripts can support an offline evaluation but cannot intercept delivery.

Agree how drafts and source versions are identified, how decisions are recorded, and the latency and cost budget. Refund execution, payments, account changes and other action permissions remain separate controls.

What needs to be tested?

Missing or conflicting evidence, changed drafts, scorer timeouts, duplicate events, model changes and new request types. Agree a review fallback and verify it before activation.

The walkthrough makes these checks concrete. A real integration needs its own compatibility assessment and tests.

A basis for a deployment decision.
