Evidence & evaluation

Control the errors you define in your scribe’s notes.

Medication and allergy fidelity provide one example. Other documentation errors require a matching reference, risk definition and evaluation.

Source fidelity and clinical truth

The walkthrough checks whether an original draft preserves medication and allergy facts in the supplied encounter source. A faithful note can still reflect an incomplete or incorrect source. Clinical interpretation and final sign-off remain with clinicians.

The additional-checking comparison

In the example of 1,000 notes, 400 additional structured checks and 60 audits remain. That is 540 fewer additional checks than checking every draft. All notes still require clinician review and sign-off.

The default 2% limit concerns target errors in routine-eligible original drafts before audit. The example records 6 such errors among 600 eligible drafts. These are prepared scenario counts, not a calculated bound, clinical validation or measured saving.

What an evaluation establishes

Define the note type, source and errors with your clinical partners. Compare the existing review process against the proposed process using independent references. Measure retained errors, additional checks, audits, correction effort and clinician time.

Evaluate source quality, language, specialty, template and model versions. Preserve original drafts separately from corrected notes, and keep policy selection separate from evaluation.

Missing source, unsupported language, changed versions or unavailable assessment must retain a defined review path. EyeTrustAI does not write a signed record or replace the clinician’s treatment decisions.

Can existing reviews be reused? How much evidence is needed?

Existing reviews may help if they retain the original draft, the source available at drafting, compatible versions, the agreed error definition and traceable reviewer decisions. Corrected or signed notes alone do not reveal the original error.

Assess source quality, missing cases, selection bias, related visits and separation from scorer development before deciding how evidence can be used. Do not silently treat prior labels as fresh calibration evidence. Changed languages, specialties, note formats or encounter mixes need a scope review; evidence does not automatically transfer.

There is no universal label count or deployment time. Requirements depend on error prevalence, the chosen limit, scorer quality, dependence, reference quality and intended use. In the demo, archived labels are not counted toward a new round.

What remains after activation?

Your product team monitors versions, language, source quality and encounter mix. Assigned clinical reviewers carry out audits and investigate findings. Unchanged models can still face changed populations.

Audit findings arrive after the affected work. A score shift is a prompt to investigate, not proof of an error rate; missed or delayed signals are possible. Agree when reduced checking must stop, who reviews potentially affected notes and what evidence is required to resume.

On suspension, new work returns to additional checking in the existing clinical workflow. Original sources, drafts, review decisions and signed records remain available under the agreed retention policy. A clinical owner decides whether earlier records need review or amendment; the demo does not recall, overwrite or silently correct them.
