What exactly would the guarantee cover?

The error: an original extraction with at least one wrong or missing critical field, judged against the source invoice and the agreed definition. Each affected invoice counts once.

The population: the original extractions selected by the policy as eligible to skip capture checking, before random audits or corrections. The error rate among the final unchecked invoices is a separate quantity.

The statement: a statistical bound on the defined error risk in that selected population, using a procedure that accounts for policy selection. It does not guarantee every invoice or a fixed error count in each batch, and it does not bound euros lost.

The conditions: relevant reviewed evidence, an appropriate scorer, compatible versions and future invoices consistent with the assessment’s assumptions. Changed suppliers, layouts, languages, entities or models may require reassessment. Some targets support no useful reduction in review.

Extraction fidelity concerns what the invoice says. Tax-law correctness, authenticity, fraud, coding, matching, approval and payment require their own controls.

Proposed integration pattern. Existing business holds remain authoritative. The walkthrough simulates routing and does not connect to a live invoice API or ERP.

What would we need to connect?

Source documents and original extracted fields, stable IDs and versions, a review route and a point before capture handoff. Corrections must remain separate from the original output so that the evidence still records the extractor’s mistakes.

Agree scoring location, source access, throughput, latency, retention and integration costs during the evaluation.

What happens when conditions change?

Retain capture review on unavailable scoring, missing inputs, incompatible versions or a suspended policy. Agree how changes in invoice populations are investigated and when new evidence is required.

Retries and reprocessing need consistent decisions, audit selection and duplicate-handoff prevention.

A concrete first step

Evaluate one invoice workflow
against your current controls.

Start retrospectively with original extractions and source invoices. Agree the risk target and what would make a change worthwhile before changing the live process.

What you bring

One workflow. Its evidence.

  • Source invoices and the original, uncorrected extracted fields with their versions.
  • Review history and reference labels, including correct cases as well as errors.
  • Current flags, rules, review volumes and time spent checking data.
  • Critical fields, acceptable risk and the initial document population.
  • A product or integration owner and an AP reviewer.

What the evaluation delivers

A basis for a deployment decision.

  • The supported error-risk bound and its scope, where evidence permits one.
  • A comparison of current controls, the scorer and calibrated policy on held-out invoices.
  • Retained checks, audits and useful automation at the chosen limit.
  • Net checking effort and cost, with setup and reassessment shown separately.
  • Integration requirements and a recommendation on a monitored pilot or further preparation.

The result may support worthwhile automation, call for more evidence or show that capture review should remain. No fixed label count, saving or deployment time is assumed.
