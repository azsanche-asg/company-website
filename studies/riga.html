A model can transfer while its calibration does not.

What we tested

The frozen Chákṣu model was applied to RIGA. Six expert contours were reconstructed per eligible image. Of 750 originals, 679 met the reference-extraction rule. Final local calibration used 181 images, with 182 separate evaluation images for both transferred and local policies.

At local targets of 1%, 5%, 10% and 20%, observed omission was 0.21%, 3.45%, 6.88% and 15.48%. Mean image area was 8.91%, 1.77%, 0.59% and 0.40%, respectively. The risk budget changed the spatial output substantially.

What the evidence supports

The RIGA-local adjusted conservative calibration quantity was 4.9964%, meeting the 5% expected omission target. The source certificate did not formally transfer to RIGA.

The statistical statement, in more detail

The method controls expected loss across calibration samples and a new example under its exchangeability assumptions, with a fixed model and a bounded, monotone loss family. It is not a 95% confidence bound for each fitted rule. A finite evaluation can exceed the chosen target.

Observed evaluation error, calibration support and practical output size measure different things. The study’s declared population and reference determine how its result should be read.

Scope of the result

The guarantee averages over images and six expert references. It is not patient-level, source-specific or worst-expert control. The 23-image Magrabi slice had about 10.35% observed omission. This retrospective research comparison establishes neither clinical safety nor that recalibration replaces model adaptation. No source retinal images are redistributed here.

Sources & data

The public collection documents methods and reproduces calibration calculations and aggregate reports. It does not reconstruct every historical model prediction from raw data.
