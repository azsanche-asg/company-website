More evidence makes calibration more stable.

Consumer-injury records test both a final error target and the stability of policies built with different amounts of calibration data.

Calibration can work with little evidence yet vary greatly from sample to sample. More labels made the operating policy substantially more reproducible.

The experimentResultsStatistical supportScopeSources & data

What we tested

A categorical logistic model trained on 2023 CPSC injury records predicted high- or low-acuity eventual emergency-department disposition. Warm-up used 2024 records. Final calibration used 1,000 records from 2025, with a separate 10,000-record evaluation. Survey weights and narratives were not used.

Across repeated warm-up runs, the 5th–95th percentile width of the chosen threshold contracted by 90.2% between 20 and 1,000 calibration records. Formal support at 20 records did not imply a stable policy or a universal label requirement.

What the evidence supports

Primary calibration recorded 49 misses among 1,000 records. The finite-sample adjustment was (49 + 1) / (1,000 + 1) = 4.995%, meeting the 5% expected set-miss target.

The statistical statement, in more detail

The method controls expected loss across calibration samples and a new example under its exchangeability assumptions, with a fixed model and a bounded, monotone loss family. It is not a 95% confidence bound for each fitted rule. A finite evaluation can exceed the chosen target.

Observed evaluation error, calibration support and practical output size measure different things. The study’s declared population and reference determine how its result should be read.

Scope of the result

This is retrospective disposition prediction using recorded assessment fields, not arrival-time triage. The evaluation’s descriptive 95% Wilson interval was 4.52–5.37%. The guarantee is not nationally weighted, class-specific or a statement of patient safety. The fixed scorer reached its training iteration cap without convergence.

Sources & data

The public collection documents methods and reproduces calibration calculations and aggregate reports. It does not reconstruct every historical model prediction from raw data.
