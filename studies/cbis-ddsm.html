What we tested

A frozen EfficientNet-B0 scored known abnormality crops. A participant was malignant if any accepted abnormality had a malignant pathology label. The study used 455 model-development participants, separate 200/200 warm-up roles, and separate 285/285 final-calibration and evaluation roles.

More calibration examples reduced policy variability in warm-up. The 5th–95th percentile width of the threshold contracted 86.18% between 20 and 150 participants. This is a study-specific stability finding.

What the evidence supports

The 5% calibration recorded 13 misses among 285 participants. Its adjusted quantity was 14 / 286 = 4.895%, meeting the expected set-miss target.

The statistical statement, in more detail

The method controls expected loss across calibration samples and a new example under its exchangeability assumptions, with a fixed model and a bounded, monotone loss family. It is not a 95% confidence bound for each fitted rule. A finite evaluation can exceed the chosen target.

Observed evaluation error, calibration support and practical output size measure different things. The study’s declared population and reference determine how its result should be read.

Scope of the result

An earlier tooling task exposed limited outcome-bearing participant-manifest information. The model and policy were not adapted using it; the result is nevertheless retrospective benchmark evidence, not a pristine prospective-style test. The primary descriptive Wilson interval was 2.42–7.21%. Known-abnormality classification is not mammography screening or lesion detection.

Sources & data

The public collection documents methods and reproduces calibration calculations and aggregate reports. It does not reconstruct every historical model prediction from raw data.
